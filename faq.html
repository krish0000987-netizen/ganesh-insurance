Insurance Guide & Frequently Asked Questions

Browse authoritative, transparent answers to 25+ essential questions covering Life, Health, General Insurance, waiting periods, claims, and policy regulations.

Insurance is a legally binding risk-management contract between an individual or enterprise (the policyholder) and an insurance provider. In exchange for regular or single premium payments, the insurer undertakes to indemnify or pay a predefined sum to the insured party upon the occurrence of covered perils, illness, disability, loss of property, or death.
Life insurance is a contract wherein the insurer guarantees payment of a designated death benefit to named beneficiaries in the event of the insured person's passing during the active policy term. It guarantees economic continuity for surviving family dependents.
Term insurance is pure life protection without an investment or cash-value accumulation element. It covers you for a set duration (e.g., 25 or 35 years). If death occurs during the term, the entire sum assured is disbursed. If the policyholder survives the term, no maturity value is returned unless a specific Return of Premium (TROP) rider is selected.
Health insurance is an indemnity coverage that reimburses or directly pays network hospitals for inpatient hospitalization, surgeries, ICU fees, daycare procedures, and specified pre- and post-hospitalization medical bills up to the purchased sum insured.
A waiting period is a defined timeframe following policy inception during which specific medical claims are not payable. Standard health policies enforce:
• Initial Waiting Period: First 30 days (accidental emergencies are covered immediately).
• Specific Disease Waiting Period: Typically 12 to 24 months for named conditions like hernia, cataract, or joint replacement.
• Pre-Existing Disease (PED) Waiting Period: Usually 24 to 36 months for conditions declared at policy proposal (diabetes, hypertension, thyroid).
Exclusions are medical conditions, events, or perils that the insurance contract expressly does not cover under any circumstances. Common health exclusions include cosmetic surgery, self-inflicted injuries, experimental or unproven treatments, and non-medical hospital consumables (unless a consumables add-on is attached).
A premium is the stipulated consideration paid periodically (annually, half-yearly, quarterly, or monthly) by the policyholder to maintain the insurance contract in active force. Failure to pay within the allowable grace period leads to policy lapse.
A deductible is a predefined threshold that the policyholder must pay out-of-pocket before the insurer begins paying for covered expenses. In Super Top-Up health plans, the deductible can be satisfied through an employer group policy or a personal base health policy.
A co-payment is a contractual percentage (e.g. 10%, 20%) of each approved claim amount that the policyholder agrees to bear out-of-pocket. Plans with mandatory co-pays generally charge lower annual premiums, but result in higher expenses during hospitalizations.
A room-rent capping restricts the daily hospital room tariff payable (e.g. 1% of sum insured per day). If a policyholder chooses a room exceeding this limit, insurers apply a proportionate deduction across surgeon fees, anesthesia, and medical procedures, leading to substantial unexpected out-of-pocket deductions. Opting for 'No Room Rent Capping' is strongly recommended.
Standard proposal documentation across Indian insurers involves:
• Identity Proof: PAN card, Aadhaar card, or Passport.
• Address Proof: Aadhaar, Passport, or recent Utility bill.
• Income Proof: (Mandatory for high-value term plans) Recent 3 months' salary slips, 6 months' bank statements, or latest 2-3 years' ITR / Form 16.
• Medical Disclosures: Details of previous surgeries, medications, or diagnostic reports if applicable.
In a network hospital, the hospital's TPA / insurance helpdesk submits a pre-authorization request to your insurance company. Upon initial approval, treatment proceeds without upfront room and procedure payment by the patient. Upon discharge, final itemized bills are settled directly between the insurer and hospital, with the patient paying only non-admissible expenses.
If treatment occurs at a non-network hospital, the patient settles the hospital bill upon discharge. Subsequently, the original discharge summary, itemized pharmacy bills, diagnostic reports, and payment receipts are submitted to the insurer within 15–30 days. The insurer verifies documentation and transfers approved funds directly to the policyholder's bank account.

• In Health Insurance: NCB is a cumulative bonus that increases your base sum insured by 10%–50% for every claim-free year without any increase in premium (up to 100% or 200% of original cover).
• In Motor Insurance: NCB is a discount (ranging from 20% to 50%) applied directly to the own-damage premium component for every consecutive accident-free renewal year.
Standard motor insurance policies deduct substantial depreciation on replaced parts during collision repairs (up to 50% on plastic, rubber, and glass parts). A Zero-Depreciation (Bumper-to-Bumper) add-on waives this deduction, ensuring the insurer bears the full cost of replacement parts.
Third-party insurance is legally mandatory under the Indian Motor Vehicles Act. It exclusively covers bodily injury, disability, death, or property damage caused to third parties. It does not cover repair expenses to your own vehicle. Comprehensive insurance covers both third-party liability AND damage to your own vehicle caused by accidents, fire, theft, or natural calamities.
Yes, under IRDAI guidelines, health insurance portability allows policyholders to switch from their existing insurer to a new insurer without losing accrued waiting-period credits for pre-existing diseases and specific ailments. Portability requests must be initiated at least 45 days prior to policy renewal.
IRDAI mandates a 15-day (or 30-day for electronic/online policies) Free Look Period from the date you receive your policy documents. During this timeframe, you may review policy terms. If unsatisfied with the terms or disclosures, you can return the policy for a premium refund (subject to deductions for proportionate risk cover, stamp duty, and medical examination expenses).
A grace period is a defined period (usually 15 days for monthly mode and 30 days for annual/half-yearly modes) granted after the premium due date to pay renewal dues without losing policy continuity. However, if a claim occurs during the grace period before premium payment, the insurer may deduct the outstanding premium before settling the claim.
If renewal premium is not paid within the grace period, the policy lapses and all coverage benefits cease immediately. In life insurance, revival may be permitted within 2–5 years subject to interest charges and proof of continued good health. In health insurance, lapsing forfeits accrued waiting period credits and cumulative bonuses.
Yes, you can hold multiple health policies (for instance, an employer-provided group policy and a personal individual policy). In the event of a high hospital bill, you can claim up to the sum insured with the primary insurer and utilize the second policy to settle any remaining hospital expenses up to its policy limit.
Bharat Griha Raksha is an IRDAI-standardized residential property insurance policy. It protects home structure and residential contents against fire, explosion, earthquake, storm, cyclone, flood, inundation, landslide, and impact damage. It automatically covers general contents up to 20% of structure sum insured (up to ₹10 Lakh) without itemized declaration.
Always verify: (1) Sum Insured adequacy against real living/medical inflation, (2) Detailed exclusions, (3) Length of pre-existing disease waiting periods, (4) Presence of room-rent sub-limits or mandatory co-payments, (5) Cashless network hospital or garage availability in your vicinity, and (6) Accurate disclosure of your personal medical history in the proposal form.
Insurance is based on the legal principle of 'Utmost Good Faith' (Uberrima Fides). Concealing pre-existing medical conditions, smoking habits, or existing treatments can give insurers legal grounds to reject claims or cancel the contract under non-disclosure provisions. Total upfront transparency protects your claim validity.
